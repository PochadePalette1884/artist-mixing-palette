    Each page holds one hue, with value rising from 1 to 9 and chroma stepping out by 2 from the neutral. The chips are worked out from the notation for the screen, so a page shows only what a screen can display; the colors are a guide, not a substitute for a physical Munsell book.

    Paint mixes by subtraction. Each pigment absorbs part of the spectrum, and the mix keeps only the light that all of its pigments reflect. The wavelength it registers as is the single spectral color the eye matches its hue to. Purples have no single wavelength, so they are named by their complement, marked with a c.

    Colors come from a spectral pigment model (Kubelka–Munk mixing over 31 wavelength bands), so mixtures darken and dull the way paint does rather than averaging like light. Oleogel is modeled as a perfectly clear medium: it adds no color, it only spreads the pigment thinner in a film of fixed thickness, so the ground shows through. Hiding is measured the way a drawdown card does it, by comparing the film over black with the film over white. The reachable-colors cloud samples every pair of pigments at many ratios, every set of three (when the set is small enough to compute quickly) and several amounts of white, all at full strength. Munsell notation is estimated from CIELAB and rounded to 2.5 hue steps; real tubes vary by brand, oil and load, so treat the numbers as a guide and check against a physical Munsell chart.
